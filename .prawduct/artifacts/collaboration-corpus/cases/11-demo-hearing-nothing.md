# "Shouldn't I be hearing something?"

## User said (queued)

> shouldn't I be hearing something?

## Context

Forty-five minutes after the agent's design pitch ended *"Let me get building."* Twelve tracks and their device chains had been loaded into Live. Zero notes existed.

## Agent did

*"No — and you shouldn't be. Zero notes exist."* Correct, and a little late.

## What happened next

The user's first musical reaction of the session came at +65 minutes — *"so... it's a decent first start"* — and with it a request that names the missing mechanism exactly:

> Can you capture a mp3 or flac of the current state, save it in the song directory, with some kind of index to this moment in chat?

It was never built; the session ended two hours in with the song never rendered.

## The tell

The build order (chains for twelve tracks before a single note) optimized for the artifact's completeness, not for the moment the user could react. Where audio *did* flow, in a later session, the user's steering became dense and specific within minutes (CTM-09's successor turns: *"measure 34 is abrupt"*, *"the guitar is totally gone"*, *"lacking feel"*).

## The right move

One part, one section, pushed and audible, before the palette for twelve tracks. The user's own ask — a progress take with an index into the conversation — is the hearing rhythm made durable.

## Assertion

Time-to-first-audio is bounded by one hearable unit, not by the size of the song. A request for a progress render is honored before more is authored.

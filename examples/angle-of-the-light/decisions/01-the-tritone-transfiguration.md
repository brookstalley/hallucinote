## Context

The brief asks for a chord change into the chorus where "the grim evaporates, the
sun rises, the ominous notes are suddenly major somehow." That `somehow` is the
only place the brief names a destination without a mechanism, and it is load-bearing
for the whole piece: the semantic arc is "we start with doubt, we claw our way to
success, but we can never escape doubt."

The brief also names the failure mode to avoid — synth demos, "technically
impressive and artistically void." Diagnosis: void is not caused by too many
events. It is caused by every event being NEW, so nothing accumulates. That
matters here because the brief explicitly WANTS chaos ("varied, constantly
changing. crazy; anything could happen"). Restraint is therefore the wrong
antidote. The right one is unity underneath — every surface change a
transformation of one object.

## Decision

The intro's most stressful chord and the chorus's most radiant chord are **the
same five pitches**. Only the bass moves, and it moves by tritone.

    intro  (doubt)      D  F  Ab  C  Eb        = D half-diminished, flat 9
    chorus (triumph)    Ab C  Eb  F  D         = Ab major 6 add sharp-11 (Lydian)

The intro layers one pitch per bar to arrive at the first voicing: D, +A, +F, +C,
+Eb, +Ab. Six layers over six bars — the brief's "starts simple and layers more and
more harmony to modulate through common to an uncommon and stressful chord",
arriving on a chord containing both a tritone (D-Ab) and a minor ninth (D-Eb).

At the chorus the bass drops a tritone to Ab and the voicing re-spells. Nothing is
transposed. The melody's motif arrives **untransposed** over the new bass, sitting
as sharp-11 / 6 / 5.

At the crescendo the bass slides back Ab -> D over one bar. The chord does not
change. The triumph is not destroyed — it is **revealed**.

## Why

Three things fall out of one mechanism, which is why it is worth more than a
clever modulation:

1. It answers the brief's `somehow` with something specific and demonstrable.
2. It makes the ending inevitable rather than appended. The brief asks for a
   crescendo that "somehow morphs into a minor key" — here it does not morph, it
   is unmasked, which is a stronger and more honest gesture.
3. It supplies the unity that lets the surface be as chaotic as the brief wants.
   Every genre lurch, meter change and instrumentation swap is a variation on one
   five-note object, so the piece accumulates instead of merely varying.

The alternative considered and rejected was a chromatic-mediant arrival (bridge
descends to F, chorus lands on Db major sharing the pitch F). It is surprising and
bright, but it is a different chord — it makes the chorus a new place rather than
the same place seen from another angle, which is exactly the narrative claim.

## Narrative to sound

The song's thesis is that we never escape doubt. Most settings of that idea have
doubt RETURN — it leaves, then comes back. This setting says something harder and
truer: it never left. The triumph was the same five notes the whole time; we had
only found the angle where they looked like victory, and the crescendo is the
angle shifting back. That is why the title is *The Angle of the Light*, and why
the outro's collapse needs no new material — stripping C to Cb yields D fully
diminished, and the octave drop takes it under.

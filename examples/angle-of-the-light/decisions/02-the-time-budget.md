## Context

The brief specifies a 45-60 second piece and then names its own section lengths: a
5-10 second intro, a 28-beat verse, "a riser", a "brief 2 measure, 5/8 bridge",
4 measures of march, 4 more of doubled-down triumph, and a crescendo-to-collapse
"over one 4/4 measure."

Costed at 144 BPM, that structure totals **~41 seconds** — under the brief's own
45-second floor. The time was already there and unspent.

## Decision

Spend the surplus on the three moments that had none, and nowhere else.

    section          brief          shipped     reason
    riser            ~1 measure     2 measures  1.7 s is a blip, not production
    5/8 bridge       2 measures     4 measures  2 chords cannot read as a descent
    outro            1 measure      2 measures  4 events in 1.7 s reads as a glitch

Everything else is exactly as specified. Final total: 110 beats at 144 BPM =
**45.8 seconds**, inside the brief's window.

## Why

Each of the three was starved in a way that would have destroyed the thing it
existed to do.

**The bridge.** The brief asks for "descending harmony, grim." Descent is a
relationship between at least three points; two chords are just a move. Four bars
of 5/8 buy the lament tetrachord D-C-Bb-A — the oldest grim gesture in Western
music, unmistakably descending — and give the raw four-track production contrast
time to register as a production choice rather than a dropout.

**The outro.** One 4/4 bar at 144 is 1.67 seconds, and the brief asks it to carry
four distinct events: crescendo, turn to minor, turn to diminished, and an octave
pitch bend under a fade. That is 0.4 s each. More importantly this is the song's
thesis (see 01-the-tritone-transfiguration), and the reveal only lands if the
listener HEARS that the chord did not change — which needs the Ab->D bass slide to
sit before the collapse starts. Two bars: bar 1 reveals, bar 2 collapses.

**The riser.** A riser is a production showpiece by the brief's own framing
("a riser showing production ability"). Under two seconds there is no room for the
filter sweep, the accelerating snare roll and the noise build to be separately
audible.

## Narrative to sound

The brief's own theme is compression — "so much experience, such a short time we
never really know what's next." That argues for density, not for starving the
turning points. The three expanded moments are precisely the hinges: the promise
(riser), the mechanical despair (bridge), and the revelation (outro). A hinge that
moves too fast to be seen does not read as a hinge; it reads as an edit.

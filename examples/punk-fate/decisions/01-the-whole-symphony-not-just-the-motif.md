# Which Beethoven's-5th harmony gets crammed in

**Question.** "The chord progression of Beethoven's 5th" could mean the famous
first-movement material (C minor, the fate cell, the E-flat second theme) or the
whole symphony's four-movement journey.

**Answer.** The whole arc: C minor → E-flat → A-flat → C minor → dominant
transition → **C major**.

**Who decided.** agreed-after-confirm (offered as the recommended option against
a first-movement-only alternative; user chose the arc).

**Rationale.** The Fifth's actual subject is not the four notes, it's what
happens to them — a C-minor symphony that fights its way to a C-major finale.
Looping the mvt-I material for two minutes would give you a good punk song about
a famous riff. Compressing the journey gives you a punk song that *is* the
symphony's argument: it starts in the dark, it gets one early glimpse of the
light (the C-major blaze inside the A-flat break — Beethoven's own foreshadow),
it's dragged back into the scherzo, and it earns the major key through a
crescendo. Two minutes is enough for a narrative if every section is short; it's
not enough for a narrative *and* a repeat.

**Consequence.** No section repeats its neighbour's key. There is no second
chorus — the C-major finale takes the structural slot a second chorus would
occupy, which is why the song can go somewhere in 115 seconds.

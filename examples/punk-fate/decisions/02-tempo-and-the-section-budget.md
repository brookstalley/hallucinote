# 200 BPM, and what that buys

**Question.** How fast, and how do eight sections fit in two minutes?

**Answer.** 200 BPM, 4/4. One bar = 1.20 s. 96 bars = 115.2 s.

**Who decided.** agreed-after-confirm (200 recommended against 176 and 230).

**Rationale.** 200 is fast enough to be unambiguously punk and slow enough that
the A-flat break and the dominant-pedal bridge still register as contrast — at
230 they blur into the surrounding sections and the song loses its shape. It
also sits near Beethoven's own marked *Allegro con brio* pulse for the first
movement, which is a joke that only works if nobody has to be told.

The budget is the reason the arc is possible at all:

| Section | Bars | Seconds |
|---|---|---|
| intro-fate | 8 | 9.6 |
| verse-cm | 16 | 19.2 |
| chorus-eb | 12 | 14.4 |
| break-ab | 12 | 14.4 |
| scherzo-cm | 16 | 19.2 |
| bridge-pedal | 8 | 9.6 |
| finale-cmaj | 16 | 19.2 |
| coda | 8 | 9.6 |

**Consequence.** The finale gets 16 bars — the same as the verse and the scherzo.
That is deliberate: a payoff shorter than the sections that set it up reads as a
tag. Conversely the bridge gets only 8, because a crescendo that outlasts its
own tension stops being a crescendo.

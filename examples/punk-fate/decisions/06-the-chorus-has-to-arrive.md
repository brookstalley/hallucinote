# Subtract from the verse; let the gang vocal be the arrival

**Question.** The first compositional pass put the verse and the chorus at an
identical 49 notes/bar. The brief says the chorus is the relative-major *lift*
and the finale is the *destination*. Neither was true on paper.

**Who decided.** inferred at `/compose-review`, applied rather than deferred —
it implements a brief row that was already DECIDED, it does not decide anything
new. One line reverts each half.

**What changed.**

1. **Verse bars 1–8: the guitar plays quarters, not 8ths.** The bass keeps its
   8ths the whole way, so the engine never stops — the *guitar* arrives at bar 9.
   Verse now reads 37 → 49 across its own sixteen bars instead of sitting flat.
   This is subtraction, the first move in the fix order, and it cost the song
   nothing: the section that felt "full" felt full because nothing had been
   saved.
2. **The chorus doubles the vocal at the octave**, and **the finale brings that
   doubling back**. A room shouting along is what a punk chorus actually is, and
   it widens the *register* rather than just adding note count — which is what
   makes a chorus read as opening up rather than continuing.

**The resulting arc** (notes/bar): 30 → 43 → **53** → 18 → 43 → 34 → **53.5** →
36. The break is the deep trough it was designed to be, the chorus lifts, and
the finale is the peak — narrowly, which is right: the finale should out-rank
the chorus, not humiliate it.

**Rationale.** With only four parts and every part playing from bar 9, the song
had no subtraction left to spend. The instinct at that point is to make the
chorus louder; the cheaper and better move is to make the verse smaller. Nothing
about the chorus's own notes changed except the octave double.

**What was NOT done, and why.** The chorus briefly also got a kick pickup — it
pushed the chorus *above* the finale, which inverts the song's whole argument.
Taken back out. See [[../attempts/2026-08-10-chorus-density-parity-with-verse]].

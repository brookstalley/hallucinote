# Intro / outro feel — frame, not flavor

The intro and outro derive from the verse, not from a fourth flavor. They're the song's *frame* — bookends that prepare entry and earn exit.

## Production cues

- Verse drum pulse stripped to hi-hat + sparse kick
- Verse pad swelling in/out, no melody
- One sustained bass D
- A single bell tone for color
- **Synth lead echoes the vocal hook** (chiptune-flavored — square/pulse, slight bitcrush). Pre-establishes the chiptune vocabulary before the voice enters; the listener thinks "oh, *that's* what that melody was" when vocals arrive.

## Foreshadowing strategy

See `decisions/2026-05-01-intro-foreshadows.md` for the deliberate move: the intro previews all three rhythmic identities, the verse harmonic descent, and the vocal melody — so the listener feels each section "earned" when it arrives.

The outro is the inverse: it strips back to the same minimal palette and lets the chiptune fade carry the listener out.

# Voice leading conventions

How the pad chord voicings move across each section.

## Verse pad

- F3/A3/D4 (Dm) → G3/B♭3/D4 (Gm) → F3/B♭3/D4 (B♭) → A3/C#4/E4 (A)
- D4 is the common tone across bars 1-14 — it anchors the descent.
- v2 added: top voice walks D4→E4→F4→E4 across bars 1-8 of the long Dm (the held chord doesn't have to feel static).

## Chorus pad

- Standard major7/min7 voicings: Dm7 / Fmaj7 / Cmaj7 / G (or the C3' twist substitution).
- Top voice moves smoothly — avoid leaps when a step works.

## C3' twist (the climax)

Last 2 bars swap G triad → B♭maj7 voicing F3/A3/B♭3/D4. One half-step move from C E G B (the C3 standard form), so the substitution voice-leads smoothly back to Dm via D and F common tones.

See `decisions/2026-05-01-c3-prime-twist.md` for the strategic reason.

## Reusable principle

Common tones across chord changes are the cheapest way to make voice-leading feel intentional. When a chord shifts, hold what you can; move only what must move. The verse pad's D4 across 14 bars is the example.

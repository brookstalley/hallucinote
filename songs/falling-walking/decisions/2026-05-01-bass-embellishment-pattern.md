# Bass embellishment pattern: plain / embellished / walk-in

The v3 musicality pass settled on a 3-bar-rotation embellishment grammar for synth bass across the song:

- **Plain bar** — establishes the chord (root, possibly chord tones on strong beats)
- **Embellished bar** — develops with chord-tone motion (3rds, 5ths, octave-up answers, ghost notes)
- **Walk-in bar** — chromatic or scalar walk that *prepares the next chord change*

Each 2-bar chord block becomes a mini phrase rather than a static drone. No two bars in the verse repeat verbatim.

## Manifestations in falling-walking

**Verse bass** (D → G → B♭ → A descent over 15 bars):
- Bars 1-8 (long Dm): rotates plain / embellished / walk through the 8-bar holding pattern with octave-up answers on bar 4 of every other 2-bar block.
- Walk-ins:
  - F#2 → Gm at bar 9 (semitone below G2)
  - G#2 → A2 → B♭1 → B♭ chord at bar 13 (chromatic ascent into B♭, then drop into B♭ register)
  - B♭1 → A1 → A chord at bar 15 (semitone resolution)
- Bar 15: 8th-note drive into chorus.

**Chorus bass** (D-F-C-G, 8 bars):
- Odd bars (1/3/5/7) = plain tresillo on root — establishes the calypso pulse.
- Even bars (2/4/6/8) = embellished with chord-tone (3rd) → root → 5th-up octave → root → walk-to-next-chord.

## Why this works

- **Listener orientation**: the plain bar tells you "we're on chord X." The embellishment says "and here's some flavor." The walk-in says "movement is coming." The grammar is teachable to the ear within a few bars.
- **Variation without chaos**: each bar has a role, so embellishment never feels random.
- **Forward motion**: walk-ins generate harmonic anticipation cheaply — much more effective than rhythm-only variation.

## Reusable

This is the *bass voice grammar* for the whole song. Future songs in similar genres can borrow it directly. When generating bass parts, structure as plain/embellished/walk-in rotation rather than thinking in single-bar units.

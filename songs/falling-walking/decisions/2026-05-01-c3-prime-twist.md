# C3' twist — last 2 bars sub G → B♭maj7

The third chorus (C3') swaps its last 2 bars from a G triad to **B♭maj7** instead of repeating the standard chorus cadence.

## Why

- **Ties the whole song together.** B♭ appears in verse VI and is the bridge's tonic. With this swap, *every section* shares B♭ — the substitution makes B♭ a structural pillar rather than a passing chord.
- **Breaks the chorus's ascending-fifths cycle.** Standard chorus loops D-F-C-G (four ascending fifths = the "Escher's stairs" infinite climb). C3' shatters that illusion at the song's emotional peak: D-F-C-**B♭**.
- **Voice-leads beautifully back to Dm.** D and F are common tones between B♭maj7 and Dm — the song re-enters the tonic without jarring.

## Manifestations

| Track | C3 (bars 33-40) | C3' (bars 40-48) |
|-------|-----------------|------------------|
| Pad | Dm7 / Fmaj7 / Cmaj7 / G | Dm7 / Fmaj7 / Cmaj7 / **B♭maj7** (voicing F3/A3/B♭3/D4) |
| Sub bass | D-F-C-G roots | D-F-C-**B♭-1 octave** |
| Bass | tresillo on D/F/C/G | tresillo on D/F/C, **B♭ walk in bars 7-8** (Bb1 → D2 → F2 → A1) |
| Pluck | calypso tresillo on cycle | tresillo on B♭maj7 chord tones in bars 7-8 |
| Bell | high counter-melody | inherits twist via same chord-tone selection |

## What it changes

Without this decision: the third chorus is repetitive structural padding. With it: the song's climax is the moment the listener hears B♭maj7 in the chorus slot and the cycle-of-fifths illusion collapses. This is the central deliberate harmonic move of the song.

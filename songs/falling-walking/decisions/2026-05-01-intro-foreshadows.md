# Intro foreshadows everything

The 16-bar intro doesn't just establish the tempo and key — it previews **every major element** of the song so the listener feels "I've heard this before" the moment each thing arrives.

## What gets foreshadowed

1. **All three rhythmic identities** via the progressive hat journey:
   - Bars 1-4: tick on 1 (sparse, minimal)
   - Bars 5-8: bossa 16th shaker pattern (previews the bridge)
   - Bars 9-12: tresillo pattern (previews the chorus calypso cell)
   - Bars 13-16: trip-hop 8ths (the verse groove)
   - Open hat lift on bar 16 last 16th (transition into verse)

2. **The verse harmonic descent** via the chorus-pluck arp track:
   - Bars 1-4: Dm sparse (the "seeking" feel)
   - Bars 5-8: Dm 8ths (denser)
   - Bars 9-12: Gm 8ths ("iv reveal" — previews the verse's bar 9 chord change)
   - Bars 13-14: B♭ 16ths
   - Bar 15: A held (V tension)
   - Bar 16: silence

3. **The vocal hook melody** via the chiptune lead track:
   - Bars 9-12: hook fragment (4 notes)
   - Bars 13-16: full hook
   - This establishes the chiptune sonic vocabulary BEFORE vocals enter, so when vocals arrive the listener thinks "oh, *that's* what that melody was."

4. **High-register color** via the bell track (NEW in v2):
   - Bars 5-8: sparse high arpeggio (D5/F5/A5/D5) — fills the empty early intro

## Why

Foreshadowing is a craft borrowed from film scoring + classical development. The listener doesn't consciously notice the intro's preview of all three grooves and the verse's harmonic curve — they just feel like the song "earned" its arrival when each section starts. Familiarity-without-prior-exposure is the cheapest emotional move in arrangement.

## What it changes

Without intro foreshadowing: each section arrives as a surprise (which can work but isn't this song's intent). With it: each section feels inevitable — the song earns every move. The chiptune lead specifically front-loads the vocal melody so the eventual vocal arrival is a moment of recognition rather than introduction.

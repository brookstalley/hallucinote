# falling-walking

## Concept

Verse seeks; chorus finds. Verse feels like inevitable falling. Chorus feels circular — Escher's stairs, climbing forever without arriving. Bridge is a sneaky sideways step into a brighter room.

## Core specs

- **Key:** D minor (verse + chorus), B♭ major (bridge)
- **Tempo:** 132 BPM
- **Time signature:** 4/4
- **Aesthetic:** Unabashedly electronic, powerful

---

## Harmony

### Verse — "falling, seeking" (15 bars)

| Bars | Chord | Function |
|------|-------|----------|
| 1–8  | Dm    | i        |
| 9–12 | Gm    | iv       |
| 13–14| B♭    | VI       |
| 15   | A     | V        |

- Bass walks D → G → B♭ → A. The iv (Gm) adds weight before the descent — darker than a plain tetrachord.
- Harmonic rhythm accelerates (8 → 4 → 2 → 1). The floor gives way faster as the verse progresses.
- 8 bars on Dm = the "seeking" — ear hunts for movement that won't come.
- A (V) in bar 15 yanks back to Dm. No exit.

### Chorus — "circular, finding" (8 bars, 2 each)

| Bars | Chord | Function              |
|------|-------|-----------------------|
| 1–2  | Dm    | i                     |
| 3–4  | F     | III                   |
| 5–6  | C     | VII                   |
| 7–8  | G     | IV (Dorian, borrowed) |

- Root motion F → C → G → D = four ascending fifths. Cycle of fifths is an infinite staircase; the loop snaps back to Dm and you climb again.
- G (Dorian IV, B natural) brightens the chorus without modulating — the "finding."
- Equal 2-bar lengths = metronomic, circular feel.

### Bridge — "sideways step" (B♭ major, 8 bars)

| Bars | Chord    | Function |
|------|----------|----------|
| 1–2  | B♭maj7   | I        |
| 3–4  | Gm7      | vi       |
| 5–6  | Cm7      | ii       |
| 7–8  | F7       | V        |

- B♭ is already a chord in the verse — listener has tasted it. Pivoting *to* B♭ as a key center feels organic, not jarring.
- Classic bossa I-vi-ii-V loop. Sophisticated, warm, contemplative.
- **Pivot back:** F7 → Dm. F7 is the V of B♭ inside the bridge, but its 7th (E♭) resolves down to D, and F is already III in Dm. Smooth re-entry into the next verse.

### Why the three pair

- Verse bass falls overall (D–G–B♭–A); chorus bass climbs in fifths (D–F–C–G); bridge sits on a brighter parallel plane.
- Verse harmonic rhythm decelerates toward resolution; chorus is metronomic; bridge is even, smooth.
- All three share B♭ and F as common chords — same harmonic neighborhood, three different psychologies.

## Instrumentation

### Core kit (consistent across song)

- **Drums** — Drum Rack, modern producer samples. Tight punchy kick (with click + body, not 808 sub-only), crisp layered snare, hats with character. Lane 8 / RÜFÜS DU SOL / Bonobo cleanliness.
- **Sub layer** — sine sub doubling bass root for weight under everything
- **Synth bass** — Operator / Wavetable, warm with harmonic bite

### Verse — dark + falling

- **Pad:** detuned supersaw, dark low-pass filter, slow attack, subtle distortion + chorus. Oppressive, sits at the back. Optional granular layer for unease.
- **Bass:** synth bass with grit + slow portamento between root changes — D → G → B♭ → A *slides*. Portamento itself sounds like falling.
- **Texture:** vinyl crackle, sparse ride bell ghost notes, reversed swells into bar 1
- Sidechain pad to kick (subtle pump = "breathing" weight)

### Chorus — half-time + calypso, brighter

- **Pad:** same patch but filter opens up — sonic version of "finding"
- **Bass:** shorter, plucky synth bass. Less sustained, supports calypso 16ths
- **Calypso element:** glassy synth-marimba or steel-drum synth playing the 3-3-2 tresillo cell. Bright, high, wide stereo
- **Counter-melody bell synth** for sparse high-register answering phrases
- Half-time kick/snare grounds the float

### Bridge — bossa in B♭

- Drop the aggressive pad entirely
- **Electric piano (Rhodes-style)** for B♭maj7 / Gm7 / Cm7 / F7 voicings — warm, sophisticated, electronic-acoustic
- **Smooth synth bass with portamento** — fretless-ish, walking bossa lines
- Soft electronic rim + light shaker (bossa cell at quiet level)
- Maybe a single sustained pad note for low-presence atmosphere

### Intro / outro

- Verse drum pulse stripped to hi-hat + sparse kick
- Verse pad swelling in/out, no melody
- One sustained bass D
- A single bell tone for color
- **Synth lead echoes the vocal hook** (chiptune-flavored — square/pulse, slight bitcrush). Pre-establishes the chiptune vocabulary before the voice enters; the listener thinks "oh, *that's* what that melody was" when vocals arrive.

### "Powerful" production tricks

- Sidechain pad + bass to kick (subtle, not EDM pumping)
- Saturation on bass + pad for harmonic richness
- Wide stereo on pads, mono bass below ~150Hz
- Reserve transient peaks for chorus

**Why the B♭maj7 swap:** Ties the whole song together (B♭ appears in verse VI and is the bridge's tonic — every section now shares B♭). Voice leads beautifully back to Dm (D and F are common tones).

### Technique notes

- Removing the chorus pad stabs was a Live engine quirk fix: stabs at the same pitch as the sustained chord retrigger the synth's slow-attack envelope, killing the bloom. The MIDI showed an 8-beat sustain but the audible note effectively ended at the first stab.
- Bass embellishment pattern that worked here: **plain bar establishes, embellished bar develops, walk-in bar prepares next chord**. Each 2-bar chord block becomes a mini phrase rather than a static drone.
- Drummer logic: small accents (open hat, ride bell) are cheap — they imply intent without cluttering. Soft crashes (vel 70-88) on chord-change downbeats mark form for the listener; loud crashes would derail the brooding aesthetic.

### Why this order

EQ8 first on every track (HP filter pass) gives the biggest perceived clarity gain for the least effort — even at default Live settings, just having the slot present is harmless; once the HP frequencies are dialed, low-end mud disappears across the song. Sidechain is the largest single missing element for "powerful electronic" character — but it's strictly blocked on MCP req #17, so leaving comps off-track until that lands.

## MCP capability gaps observed

Surfacing these for future tooling improvements:

1. **`add_notes_to_clip` is misleadingly named** — it actually *replaces* all notes (via `clip.set_notes()`), not appends. The response message ("Added N notes") reinforces the wrong mental model. Either rename to `set_clip_notes` or change the response to "Set N notes (replaced previous)".

2. **No `add_notes_to_arrangement_clip` exposed as MCP tool** — the underlying remote script registers it (line ~240 of `__init__.py`), but it's not in the MCP tool list. This forces a delete-and-redup cycle for any session-clip change to propagate to arrangement. For 46 arrangement clips, that's 92 round-trips instead of 13.

3. **No `delete_session_clip`** — only `delete_arrangement_clip` exists. To wipe a session clip slot, you have to either accept the existing clip and call `add_notes_to_clip` (replace) or live with stale slots forever. There's no `clip_slot.delete_clip()` exposed.

4. **No `get_clip_notes`** — once notes are written, they can't be read back via API. Can't verify what's in a clip without opening Ableton manually.

5. **`load_instrument_or_effect` returns `Devices on track:` empty** — uninformative; can't programmatically confirm what loaded.

6. **`create_cue_point` errors on names containing apostrophes** — `"C3'"` failed with misleading "Cue point already exists at this position: 1". Worked with `"C3 prime"`. The error message is doubly wrong (wrong cause, wrong position).

7. **`get_cue_points` shows numeric IDs not names** — names persist in Ableton UI but the readback shows `"1"`, `"2"`, etc. Can't programmatically verify cue point names.

8. **`duplicate_clip_to_arrangement` doesn't return the new clip's index/name** — just "Duplicated session clip to arrangement on track N". Can't track which arrangement clip is which without a follow-up `get_arrangement_info`.

9. **No bulk operations** — every clip add/delete/duplicate is a separate call. For 46 arrangement clips: 46 deletes + 49 dups = 95 round-trips. A `bulk_arrangement_ops` accepting a list would be much faster.

10. **No `clear_arrangement` or `delete_all_arrangement_clips_on_track`** — delete-everything is N calls.

11. **`create_midi_track` doesn't accept a `name` parameter** — always need a separate `set_track_name` call.

12. **`get_browser_tree` shows only top level** — for `instruments` it returned just "Instruments" with no children. Required separate `get_browser_items_at_path` calls to drill in. A recursive option (e.g., `depth=2`) would save round-trips.

13. **No way to query device parameters in bulk** — `get_device_parameters` exists per-device but no enumeration of all loaded devices' params at once. Sound-design iteration would benefit from a snapshot tool.

14. **No undo / version-control / snapshot support** — once you delete arrangement clips, they're gone. No "save state" before destructive ops. Live's native undo presumably still works, but isn't exposed.

15. **Clip slots appear capped at 8 per track in default sessions** — to use slot 9+ you may need to add scenes first (untested, but `create_clip` at slot 8 worked).

- Harmonic interest (chord substitutions, 7ths, voice leading)
- Dramatic but not jarring

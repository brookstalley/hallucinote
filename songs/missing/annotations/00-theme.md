# THEME — the spine of "missing"

> **This is the north star.** Every other decision in this song — harmonic, rhythmic,
> arrangement, sound-design, mix — exists to serve the premise below. When a future
> choice is ambiguous, resolve it toward this. If a choice contradicts this, the
> choice is wrong, not the theme. Read this first; query it via `/song-context theme`.

## One sentence

A song about **loss of purpose**, rendered as a harmonic fact: the root of every
chord is missing, so the ground the listener reaches for is never there — until the
very end, when it finally arrives and the narrator is, at last, *located*.

## What the song is about

**Loss of purpose — not loss of a person.** This distinction is load-bearing.
The absence here is abstract: a missing reason, a missing direction, a self that
can't find where it stands. Not grief for someone, but the disorientation of having
nothing to orient *against*. That is why the technique fits so exactly — the *root*
is the thing a chord orients against, and it is gone.

"Found," when it comes, does **not** mean happy. It means **located** — the ground
appears under your feet; you finally know where you are. The reveal is a minor-key
resolution (E minor with its root), so arrival is relief and grounding, not joy.
Being found is being *placed*, not being *fixed*.

## The dramatic arc (lost → false summit → disabused → found)

The whole song is one withheld resolution. The listener is made to reach for a
ground that isn't there, denied it at every place pop form promises arrival, lifted
into a false belief that they've found it, dropped, and only then given the floor.

1. **Lost.** Intro and verses float with no tonal ground. The ear cannot settle.
2. **Lost at home's doorstep.** Choruses — where pop form *promises* arrival —
   keep the root missing. Full arrangement energy, zero harmonic resolution. The
   cruelest denial, because it happens at the exact moment of expected homecoming.
3. **The false summit (the break).** The song slips into its own bright **G-major
   illusion** — the phantom key that rootless E-minor has secretly implied all
   along. Euphoric, the most energetic section: *the narrator believes they have
   found their purpose.* But it is still rootless — even the false dawn has the
   wound in it. Then the pivot chord **B7** reintroduces D# (foreign to G), the
   illusion collapses, and we are deposited on the dominant of the real key.
4. **Disabused.** The last verse takes the purpose back. The summit was a lie.
5. **Found.** The reveal: the root **E** returns for the first time — a
   sub-oscillator swelling up from the empty bottom octave — as the melody lands on
   E for the first time. Voice and floor arrive together. The narrator is located.

## The one rule that makes it work

**The root is the wound. Withhold it completely, everywhere, until the reveal —
then its arrival is the entire emotional payload.** No bass note, no melody note,
no layer may supply E (the tonic root) with weight and duration before the coda.
Partial/phantom presence (overtones, a grazed passing tone) is allowed and wanted;
a *landed, grounded* root before the end would spend the only money the song has.

This generalizes to a second foundation the song may also withhold (see the rhythm
decision): **anything that functions as "the floor" should go missing with the root
and return with it.** Two missing foundations releasing at once hit harder than one.

## What this theme forbids (guardrails for future decisions)

- **No early grounding.** Don't let the bass pedal a root-sounding low note; don't
  let the melody rest on E on a strong beat with duration; don't fill the sub octave
  before the coda. (See `decisions/03-the-reveal.md`, `decisions/04-bass-approach.md`.)
- **No literal resolution in the choruses.** Their lift is arrangement energy only.
- **The break must feel like winning, then losing.** If it just feels like a
  contrasting section, it has failed — it has to be the *best* the song feels before
  it's taken away.
- **Don't soften the abstraction into a breakup song.** No "you," no person-shaped
  longing in lyric or melodic gesture. The loss is of *purpose/direction/self*.
- **"Found" stays minor.** The reveal grounds; it does not turn major or triumphant.

## Why the constraint is honest here (not a gimmick)

The title *is* the method. Most songs built on a rootless constraint have to justify
the trick. This one doesn't: the song is *about* something missing, and the thing
missing in the harmony (the root) and the thing missing in the narrator (purpose)
are the same gesture rendered in two media. Protect that alignment above all else.

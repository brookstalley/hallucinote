# 04 — Bass approach: 5ths, weightless, never pedal

**Date:** 2026-06-01
**Decided by:** agreed-after-confirm (answering user's "how do we make the bass clearly NOT the root?")
**Status:** locked

## Question

With synths, the bass register is a choice. If the lowest note reads as the root, the
concept collapses. How do we keep the bass clearly *not* the root?

## Decision — three independent forces, all used at once

1. **Play the 5th of each chord:** **B (under Em) · F# (under B7) · E (under Am).**
   A chord with its 5th in the bass is a **second-inversion (6/4)** chord, which the
   Western ear has heard as *unstable / non-resting* for centuries. The 5th-in-bass
   actively recruits instability instead of merely avoiding the root.
   - **Avoid the 3rd in the bass** — it grounds too well and gets reinterpreted as a
     root (G under Em just sounds like G major). 3rd-in-bass reinforces the phantom
     and is a tool for a *future* variation, not this one.
2. **Never pedal — keep the bass moving** (drift 5th↔7th within each chord). A
   sustained low note becomes a root by sheer persistence; motion denies the ear a
   fixed candidate to crown.
3. **High-pass ~100 Hz — present but weightless.** The bass has pitch but no floor.
   The bottom octave stays empty (`decisions/03`), so the reveal is the first real
   foundation the listener feels.

## Note on the iv chord (Am)

The 5th of Am is **E** — the tonic root. Playing E in the bass over Am is allowed
*only* because (a) it's high-passed/weightless and (b) it's the 5th of Am, not
functioning as a landed tonic. Watch this one in composition: keep it moving and
light so it doesn't pre-spend the reveal. If it ever reads as grounding, voice that
chord on its 7th (G) instead.

## Alternatives held for other variations

- **Unpitched** filtered noise / rhythmic low-end (most abstract).
- **Ambiguous drone** on a non-chord tone (a persistent tonal question mark).
- **3rds in bass** to reinforce the G-major phantom (the opposite intent).

# 06 — Melody's relationship to the root

**Date:** 2026-06-01
**Decided by:** agreed-after-confirm
**Status:** locked

## Decision

The melody **reaches for E and grazes past it** the whole song — it may touch the
tonic root in passing (scalar runs, weak beats, short durations) but **never lands on
E on a strong beat with duration** until the reveal. At the reveal (coda), the melody
**rests on E for the first time**, simultaneously with the sub-oscillator root.

## Rationale

For a song about *missing*, a melody that grazes the root but never rests on it is
the whole thesis in miniature — the "almost found," the ghost of recognition, the
thing you nearly have. Total avoidance (purer concept) is reserved for a future,
more austere variation; here the human gesture of *reaching and not quite landing*
serves "loss of purpose" better. The single landed E at the end is the melodic half
of "found." See `annotations/00-theme.md` and `decisions/03-the-reveal.md`.

## Practical note

Favor the available chord tones — 3rd, 5th, 7th, 9th — for landing notes:
- Over Em (rootless G–B–D#): land on G, B, D#, F#.
- Over B7 (rootless D#–F#–A): land on D#, F#, A, C#.
- Over Am (rootless C–E–G): land on C, G, B — **treat E here as a passing tone only**
  (it's the tonic root; don't let the melody rest on it even though it's Am's 5th).

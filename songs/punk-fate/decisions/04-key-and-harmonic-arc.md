# Key: C minor → C major arc, mirroring the symphony

## Question
What is the harmonic skeleton, and how do we map a 30-minute four-movement symphony onto a 2-minute punk arc?

## Answer
- **Verses in C minor.** Pull from Movement I's primary progressions: **i–iv–V** (Cm–Fm–G) under the fate motif, and **III–VI–iio6–V** (Eb–Ab–D°6–G) for the second-theme answer. These are the "fate, struggle" sections.
- **Choruses in C major.** Lift directly to C major, mirroring Movement IV's blazing triumph. The plagal/authentic motion (F–G–C, or IV–V–I) gives the punk chorus its release.
- **Bridge nods to Eb major.** The relative major of Cm and the key of Movement I's second theme + Movement II. A short 8-bar departure that re-tensions the listener before the final chorus.
- **Outro recaps the motif** back in C minor — the fate theme returns one more time but is interrupted, leaving ambiguity rather than resolving fully to major. Punk endings don't apologize.

## Rationale
Beethoven's 5th is famously a **darkness-to-light** journey: Cm Mvt. I (fate) → Eb-major Mvt. II (slow rest) → Cm/C scherzo Mvt. III (struggle) → C-major Mvt. IV (triumph). Condensing to 2 minutes means we can't honor each movement individually, but the *macro shape* — minor verses, major choruses, relative-major bridge, ambiguous return — preserves the arc in the shortest form that still reads.

## What this binds
- Lead guitar and staccato-synth lines target chord tones of the progressions above
- Bass plays roots of i–iv–V in verses and IV–V–I in choruses (root-only is fine in punk; the lead can hit the upper structure)
- The fate motif is a melodic constant across sections — its harmonic context shifts (Cm in verses, modal flavor in chorus where it lands on C major), the rhythm stays

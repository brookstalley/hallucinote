# Structure: intro → V → C → V → C → bridge → outro

## Question
How is the 2-minute arc divided into sections, and what does each represent?

## Answer
Seven sections, scaffolded as: `intro, verse, chorus, verse2, chorus2, bridge, outro`.

| Section  | Symbolic role                                                       | Default bars | Notes |
|----------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------|-------|
| intro    | Fate motif statement (drums + lead alone)                           | 4–8          | The "short-short-short-LONG" must be unmistakable here |
| verse    | Cm — fate/struggle (i–iv–V over the motif)                          | 16           | Full band enters; lead guitar carries the motif, synth answers |
| chorus   | C major — triumph release (IV–V–I)                                  | 16           | Synth becomes the vocal hook; lead plays power-chord stabs |
| verse2   | Cm — second theme territory (III–VI–iio6–V)                         | 16           | Harmonic detour; tension before the second chorus |
| chorus2  | C major — chorus restated, possibly with octave-up synth lift       | 16           | Bigger than chorus 1 — punk's "more" answer to repetition |
| bridge   | Eb major — relative-major departure (Mvt. II nod)                   | 8            | Single dynamic dip; can be drums-out for 4 bars then full-band re-entry |
| outro    | Cm — fate motif returns, then cuts off                              | 4–8          | No fade; punk endings are abrupt |

Total: roughly 80–92 bars. At 180 BPM that's 107–122 seconds — i.e. 1:47–2:02. Within the 2-minute target.

## Rationale
- **Two verse/chorus pairs** is the minimum that lets the chorus feel earned-then-confirmed. Skipping the second pair would make the song feel like a sketch; adding a third would blow past 2 minutes.
- **Bridge before the final chorus is conventional, but here it doubles as the Eb-major / Mvt. II nod** — the only place the song acknowledges the slow second movement.
- **Outro that recaps the motif and cuts** mirrors the symphony's habit of restating the four-note figure across movements, then ends with punk's signature "no goodbye."
- **No second-chorus-then-final-chorus pile-up** like pop arrangements use. Punk doesn't need three choruses; energy is the chorus.

## What this binds
- Compose-time bar counts can adjust ±4 bars per section to hit the 2-minute target precisely once the arrangement is in
- Cue points should mark every section start (the scaffold places these at 8-bar intervals by default; we'll fix them once final bar counts are decided)
- The fate motif (intro) is reused — same rhythm, evolving harmonic context — in every verse and outro

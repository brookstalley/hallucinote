# Genre alternation — the contrast IS the song

Reggae and metal **alternate, they never overlap**. At any given bar the song is
*either* reggae (E Dorian, half-time chill) *or* metal (E Phrygian, double-time
attack) — never both sounding at once. The whiplash between them is the joke and
the structure: "chillin in the sun zone, rasta vibes flowin in the — NO TIME FOR
THAT GOTTA GET STUFF DONE."

Section map (genre by bar):

| Bars | Section | Genre |
|---|---|---|
| 1–16 | intro + verse1 | reggae |
| 17–24 | chorus1 | metal |
| 25–32 | verse2 | reggae |
| 33–40 | chorus2 | metal |
| 41–56 | bridge | metal |
| 57–64 | outro | reggae |

## Mix implication (read this before flagging masking)

Because the genres never coexist, **cross-genre masking is not a defect** — a
mix review should never flag "the metal guitar buries the reggae skank" or "the
organ fights the gallop," because those parts are never in the same section.
Evaluate masking and balance **within a single genre's sections only**. The two
genres get deliberately different mix targets (see `mix-intent-per-section.md`):
the reggae sections want air and space, the metal sections want a dense wall.
The *abruptness* of the switch is intended — do not smooth the transitions.

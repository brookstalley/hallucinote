# Mix intent — what should win each section

The single source for "the element meant to win" that a mix review interprets
measurements against. Two mix worlds, one per genre.

## Reggae sections (intro, verse1, verse2, outro)

**Aesthetic: air and space.** The chill is made of what *isn't* there — the
dropped downbeat, the gaps between the skank chucks. Don't fill them.

- **Wins: the lead (vocal) + the off-beat skank.** The skank (`03 Rhythm Gtr`)
  on the "and" of 2 & 4 is the genre signature; the lead carries the hook.
- **Supports: bass + one-drop drums.** The `02 Bass` long-root/short-fifth
  bounce and the snare-on-3 one-drop hold the pocket. The bass should be felt,
  round, not loud.
- **Texture: organ bubble + dub space.** The Hammond bubble (`04 Organ`)
  percolates *under* the skank — it must not compete with it. The `DubDelay`
  return on snare bounces and the `Plate` tail are the dub air; generous, wet.
- **Must not happen:** the pocket sounding busy or rushed. If the reggae
  sections feel cluttered, pull the organ and the delay throws back, not the lead.

## Metal sections (chorus1, chorus2, bridge)

**Aesthetic: a dense wall of attack.** The opposite of the reggae air —
relentless, tight, loud, no gaps.

- **Wins: the gallop guitar + the lead stabs.** The palm-muted power chords
  (`03 Rhythm Gtr`, Amp = Heavy) locked to the kick gallop ARE the wall; the
  Phrygian lead ("NO TIME FOR THAT") cuts over the top.
- **Supports: the gallop drums + pedal bass.** Kick gallop + 2 & 4 snare + the
  16th pedal bass (`02 Bass`, pushed a hair ahead) are the engine. They should
  be tight and forward, glued, not spacious.
- **Reverb: dry.** The `Room` return is short/tight here — metal punch, not dub
  wash. Keep the `DubDelay` and `Plate` off the metal parts.
- **Must not happen:** the guitar/kick lock loosening, or the wall opening up
  with reverb. Density and tightness are the point; if anything, the metal
  sections should feel *more* compressed than the reggae ones.

## The Amp Type envelope is the hinge

The genre flip is audible primarily through the Amp Type envelope on
`03 Rhythm Gtr` (Clean ↔ Heavy at section boundaries). That single device move
carries the reggae→metal character change — protect it; it's the most important
automation in the song (see `decisions/05-instrumentation-and-chains.md`).

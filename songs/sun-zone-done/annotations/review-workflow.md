# Review workflow — archetype C (Subtractive / Through-Composed)

This song's declared **review-workflow archetype is C — Subtractive /
Through-Composed Art Piece** (see `.prawduct/artifacts/review-workflow-model.md`).
`/compose-review` and `/mix-review` read this and follow its discipline.

**Why C.** sun-zone-done is a 184-bar through-composed arc, not a verse/chorus
band song. It is built DENSE — two whole genres, a polyrhythm cloud, a fusion
climax — and its risk is exactly mud and over-layering (the integration *smash*
was the worked example; decisions/08). The right review move is Rubin's
*remove-until-the-identity-is-challenged*: subtract a layer, ask "did the section
lose its identity?", and if not, the subtraction was right. The ordered-pass
default (archetype A) doesn't fit — there is no "songwriting then arrangement then
mix" gate sequence here; the composition and the subtraction ARE the work.

**Axis order for this song (one axis per turn):**

1. **Arrangement / subtraction first** — for any section that feels muddy or flat,
   the first move is *remove*, not add or re-mix. (`/compose-review`, arrangement
   axis.) This is where most of the work lives.
2. **Harmony + melody** — the modal flip and the fusion chord are load-bearing;
   review them as their own pass, against the declared modes (Dorian↔Phrygian over
   a constant E). (`/compose-review`, harmony / melody axes.)
3. **Performance / feel** — the per-section feel collisions + the 1/f breathing.
   (`/mix-review`, performance axis.)
4. **Mix-balance LAST** — masking/loudness only after the arrangement is settled;
   do NOT let a fader move fix what is an over-layering problem. The integration
   ("does the fusion read as fusion, or collapse into mud?") is the section where
   this matters most — and it's an *arrangement* question first, a mix question
   second. (`/mix-review`, mix-balance axis.)

**The discipline:** a single review turn acts on ONE of these axes and defers
findings on the others (it may READ holistically, but it EDITS one axis). This is
what stops the tail-chasing the user flagged — re-mixing what is really an
arrangement problem, or re-arranging after a sound pass.

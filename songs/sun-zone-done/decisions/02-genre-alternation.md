# Genre Alternation Strategy

**Question:** How do the two genres relate within the song — overlap, blend, or alternate?

**Answer:** Strict **alternation**, not overlapping. Each genre gets to be itself fully within a phrase; the dramatic content is in the **transitions**, not in simultaneous layering. Phrase lengths nest: 4 bars reggae / 4 bars metal within verses; 2 bars / 2 bars within choruses; the intro has a single 1-bar metal interruption inside an otherwise-reggae setup; the bridge is allowed to bend this rule (genre bleed) because that's the bridge's job.

**Decided by:** user (explicit: "alternating, not overlapping")

**Rationale:**
Overlapping reggae and speed metal at the same time would either mush into incoherence (both genres are mid-range-heavy) or land as a novelty/joke (reggae skank under a metal solo = punchline). Alternation lets each genre be musically convincing on its own terms, and the *contrast at the seam* carries the song's meaning (relaxation interrupted by urgency, urgency exhausted into relaxation). The transitions become the songwriting — not throwaway moments.

The bridge is the natural place to break this rule: by then the listener has internalized the alternation, and a moment of *bleed* (e.g., metal vocal cadence over a reggae bassline, or reggae skank on the offbeats of a metal gallop) musically asks "what if these aren't actually opposites?" That's the song's emotional crux.

# Key & Modal Pivot

**Question:** What key and harmonic strategy lets reggae and speed metal coexist as one song, not a mashup?

**Answer:** Key center is **E**, same tonic throughout. Reggae sections use **E Dorian** (E F♯ G A B C♯ D); metal sections use **E Phrygian** (E F♮ G A B C♮ D). The genre flip is **two half-step shifts** on the same root: **F♯ ↔ F♮** and **C♯ ↔ C♮**.

**Decided by:** inferred and proposed, agreed by user ("yeah that is amazing")

**Rationale:**

Reggae idiomatically lives in major or Dorian-flavored minor (bright, hopeful, Mixolydian inflections in the melody). Speed metal's defining harmonic move is the **Phrygian b2** — that menacing F♮-over-E semitone clash is the genre's signature. Both modes have E as tonic; they only differ in two scale degrees. So the genre flip is a *modal* shift, not a modulation. No key change, no fight over what "the key" is — the song stays planted in E and reframes it.

This matters because:

1. **One song, two faces.** A listener perceives the tonic as constant. The genre change feels like a *mood swing*, not a key change, which matches the lyrical theme (same person, two states).
2. **The pivot has a name.** F♯→F♮ is the audible thesis. A vocal/guitar slide from F♯ down to F♮ over an E pedal *is* the WHACK transition. Reversed: F♮→F♯ relaxes the metal back into reggae.
3. **Power chords are mode-neutral.** Metal sections lean heavily on E5/F5/D5/C5 power chords, which contain no third. The mode is carried by *the lead melody and vocal* (F♮ vs F♯), not the chord voicings. This lets the rhythm guitar stay idiomatic without having to spell out a major-3rd / minor-3rd argument.

**Reggae chord palette (E Dorian):**
- Em7 / Em9 (i)
- A7 / A13 (IV — the Dorian major IV is the signature color)
- Dmaj7 (VII)
- Bm7 (v — minor, not dominant)
- Cadd9 / Cmaj7 used sparingly (foreshadows Phrygian C♮)

**Metal chord palette (E Phrygian, power chords):**
- E5 (i)
- F5 (bII — the punch)
- D5 (bvii)
- C5 (bVI)
- Occasional B5 for tension before resolving back to E5

**Forbidden:** Modulating away from E. Hard key changes would dilute the modal-pivot thesis.

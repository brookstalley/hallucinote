# Key, Tempo, Meter

**Question:** What key, what tempo, what meter?

**Answer:** **E Dorian ↔ E Phrygian**, **180 BPM**, **4/4**.

**Decided by:** Claude (in dialogue with user, 2026-05-22)

**Rationale:**

**Root: E.** Both genres love E. Reggae often sits in minor keys; metal lives in E (the lowest open string on a standard-tuned guitar — the genre's defining low end). Shared root makes the mode flip *feel* like one song shifting, not two songs spliced.

**Modes: Dorian for reggae, Phrygian for metal.**

| Mode | E-rooted notes | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **E Dorian** | E F# G A B **C#** D | The "happy minor" — natural 6th (C#) keeps it from being morose; b3 (G) gives it modal interest. Classic reggae mode (think: Bob Marley's *Could You Be Loved*). |
| **E Phrygian** | E **F** G A B **C** D | The "metal mode" — b2 (F) is the genre's harmonic trademark (the half-step above the root that gives metal its menace). |

**The harmonic joke:** Dorian has F# + C#. Phrygian has F + C. The pivot is a **double flat-second move** — F# → F (down a half step) AND C# → C (down a half step). Both Dorian's "happy notes" flip to Phrygian's "dark notes." The genre flip IS the modal flip, fully expressed.

Not a relative-mode shift, not a parallel-mode shift, not a chromatic mediant — it's the **specific two-note modification** that takes a happy minor and turns it sinister. The harmony tells the joke; the listener doesn't need the lyric to feel the shift.

**Tempo: 180 BPM.** Constant across the entire song.

- **Metal sections** inhabit 180 directly. 16th-note kick gallop = 720 attacks per minute. That's the urgency.
- **Reggae sections** feel half-time at 90 effective BPM. The notated meter is still 4/4 at 180, but the skanks land on the offbeats of beats 2 and 4 ("two-and-four-and"), and the bassline lays out a pulse on every-other beat. Listener hears 90; metronome reads 180.

This is the canonical genre-mashup trick (see: Skindred). It avoids per-section tempo changes (Live's per-section tempo automation has gaps — see `ableton://guides/gaps` Group D), keeps the underlying click constant for the band, and lets the *articulation* (not the *clock*) carry the genre.

**Meter: 4/4 throughout.** No meter changes. The contrast is genre, not time signature. Adding 7/8 metal bars or 6/8 reggae feels would muddle the joke (the listener would attribute the strangeness to rhythm not articulation).

**What this is NOT:**

- Not a key change — the root note never moves. Same fundamental.
- Not a tempo change — the BPM stays locked at 180.
- Not a meter change — 4/4 throughout.

The mode flip + articulation flip are the only two parameters that shift section-to-section. Everything else is constant. That's what makes the joke land.

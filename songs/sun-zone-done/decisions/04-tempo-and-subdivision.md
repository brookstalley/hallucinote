# Tempo & Subdivision Relationship

**Question:** Reggae sits ~70-90 BPM; speed metal sits 180+ BPM. How does the song handle tempo across alternating sections?

**Answer:** **Constant tempo of 180 BPM** throughout. Reggae sections are **felt half-time at 90 BPM** (one-drop snare/rim on beat 3 of every 2-bar pair, skanks on 2&4 of the half-time feel). Metal sections are felt straight at 180. The genre flip is in *articulation and subdivision density*, not in timing.

**Decided by:** inferred and proposed, agreed by user implicitly when accepting the harmonic plan

**Rationale:**

A literal tempo cut between reggae and metal sections would feel like a DJ mash, not a song. By holding tempo constant and changing *what's articulated*, the seam becomes musical rather than mechanical. The drummer's quarter note is the through-line; reggae plays on every other quarter (felt as half), metal plays gallops and double-kick across every 16th. Same grid, different density.

This also means:
- **No tempo automation events.** Tempo stays 180 the whole song.
- **Meter stays 4/4.** Reggae's half-time feel is achieved via the drum/bass pattern, not via meter changes.
- **Click track is unambiguous.** Live's tempo display reads 180 throughout; the producer/performer doesn't have to negotiate two tempos.
- **180 is plenty fast for "speed metal" to a non-metalhead.** Real Bay Area speed metal sits 180-220; we land at the bottom of that range. Faster would force the reggae to feel like 100+ BPM half-time, which is too perky for "chillin in the sun zone."

**Tempo: 180 BPM, 4/4, no changes.**
